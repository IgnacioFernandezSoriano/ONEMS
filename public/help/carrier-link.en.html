Carrier Link: samples from the carrier’s own records

What it is, the order to set it up in, and how the usual E2E screens and the two Carrier Link screens are used with Carrier Link.

What Carrier Link is

Until now ONEMS has measured with test shipments moved by panelists (E2E) and with RFID readings at postal centers (diagnosis). Carrier Link adds a third source: ONEMS takes shipments that are already closed from the carrier’s own records (its departure reading and its arrival reading) at random and uses them as samples.

Random samples

The carrier proposes many shipments and ONEMS makes the final draw. The draw can be reproduced in front of an auditor.

Already-closed records

Only shipments with a start reading and an arrival reading are used, because that tells us which route (city to city) they travelled.

Same reports

There are no new reports: the samples appear in the usual E2E and diagnosis reports, marked with the origin “carrier”.

Instead of an origin panelist and a destination panelist, each sample has a start reading point and an end reading point. The E2E time runs from the first reading at the start to the last at the end.

Scale. A plan can reach about 15,000 samples a year per product. That is why quotas are spread by route (city to city) and week, without you having to choose shipments one by one.
What you do not see. Only shipments that have arrived are included. Those that never arrive or stay in another city do not appear as samples, and departures are not followed up. There are no routes within a single city either. The delivery standard only affects the analysis in the reports, never the capture or the plan.
There is no separate section. Carrier Link is not a separate module: everything lives inside E2E. The only thing that changes is the capture method of the data (panelists or carrier), which is chosen per carrier in Carriers & Products: Panelists, Carrier Link or Both. The same carrier can use both systems at once. Carriers that use panelists only work exactly as before.

The order of work

  1. Capture method. In Carriers & Products, choose each carrier’s Capture method: Panelists, Carrier Link or Both. Reading points and connectors are only offered for carriers set to Carrier Link or Both.
  2. Reading points. In Reading points (menu E2E ▸ Setup ▸ Carrier Link), register the carrier places where each shipment is read and say in which city routes start or end. Each city needs at least one start point and one end point.
  3. Connector. In Connectors (same submenu), create the carrier’s connector: its field and product equivalences, the request calendar and the key. Test it with a trial batch.
  4. Delivery standard. In E2E: SLA, check that every route has its standard and, if you want to compare with panelists, the equivalence time (see The equivalence time).
  5. Generate and apply the plan. In Allocation Generator, choose the carrier with the Carrier Link capture method, generate the preview, save the plan and press Apply.
  6. Activate the connector. When everything is ready, move the connector from Testing to Active. Until then it only accepts trial runs and no sample is created.
  7. Monitoring. To spread the load better use Load Balancing; to see the quotas, Allocation Plans; in Capture monitor follow the requests, batches and alerts, and in Samples review the samples as they come in (menu Database ▸ Carrier Link).
And then: ONEMS asks the carrier for shipments on the days and at the time set in the connector calendar, makes the draw and puts the samples into the E2E reports. You only need to deal with the alerts.

Reading points

Screen Reading points. A reading point is a place (and, if wanted, a mode: acceptance, dispatch, delivery...) where the carrier reads shipments. Each point is in a postal center and therefore in a city.

  1. Choose the carrier and press Add reading point.
  2. Type the place (the code the carrier uses) and, if relevant, the mode. If you leave the mode empty, the point accepts any mode.
  3. Choose the postal center. The city comes from the center.
  4. Choose the role in the route: Start, End, Start and end, or Intermediate.
A postal center without a city can only be an Intermediate point. For a city to be the origin or destination of routes, its center must have a city.

The Places without a reading point block lists places that sent readings in the last 90 days that could not be assigned. Press Register to turn them into points.

Connectors

Screen Connectors. The connector is the channel through which the carrier delivers its data to ONEMS. There are one or more per carrier.

What is configured

  • Calendar: the days of the week and the time at which ONEMS may ask for data, so as not to disturb the carrier’s database. If you leave the time zone empty, the account’s is used.
  • Field equivalences: which carrier field holds each piece of data (shipment identifier, product, reading date and time, place and mode). While any is missing, ONEMS cannot read the data.
  • Product equivalences: which ONEMS product each carrier product value corresponds to.
  • Allowed extra fields: other carrier fields that are kept with each reading. Any other field is discarded on entry, so that no personal data is stored.

Connector statuses

StatusWhat happens
TestingIt only accepts trial batches: they are checked as if real, but nothing is saved and no sample is created.
ActiveIt receives real batches and creates samples. Activating it closes any requests that were opened during testing.
PausedIt receives no batches and no requests are sent until it is resumed.

The carrier key

The carrier uses a key to send its batches. Only a superadmin or the UPU Account Manager can create keys. Press New key, give it a name and, if you wish, an expiry date.

The key is shown only once. Copy it and give it to the carrier through a secure channel: afterwards ONEMS keeps only a prefix to recognise it. If it is lost, create another and deactivate the old one.

Testing a batch

In Test a batch (trial run) upload a batch file from the carrier. ONEMS tells you whether it would accept it and, if not, why. Nothing is saved.

Allocation Generator

Screen Allocation Generator (menu E2E ▸ Allocation). It is the same screen used with panelists: only the Capture method field changes. A plan says how many samples are wanted, for which carrier and product, on which dates and between which cities.

  1. Choose the carrier. Its capture method (the one set in Carriers & Products) decides the options. If it is Both, you choose between Panelists and Carrier Link in Capture method.
  2. With Carrier Link, also choose the connector. It is required: without it the plan cannot be applied. There are no panelists: the cities come from the carrier’s active reading points.
  3. Fill in the name, product, dates and total number of samples, and choose the distribution just as with panelists: the same A/B/C matrix, seasonality and working days. You can also import the quotas from a CSV file, with the columns origin city, destination city, week and samples.
  4. Press Generate preview. ONEMS spreads the total across routes (city to city) and weeks, and shows the result without saving it. If it could not place all the samples, it tells you.
  5. Review the preview and press Save plan (draft). The plan appears in Generated Plans, with a Capture column showing whether it is a panelist plan or a Carrier Link plan. A draft can be deleted.
  6. To start it, press Apply. With Carrier Link, applying means ONEMS starts asking the carrier for the shipments. The plan moves to Applied Plans.
If any city is missing start or end reading points, ONEMS tells you and does not let you apply the plan until you register them in Reading points. A route without a delivery standard is still sampled, but its samples enter without “on time” until the standard is created: it is only a notice.
After applying, the carrier, product, connector, start date and quotas can no longer be changed (except with Load Balancing). Samples already obtained are not touched.
And then: with the plan applied and the connector Active, the system asks for shipments by itself, week by week. There is nothing else to do.

Load Balancing

Screen Load Balancing (menu E2E ▸ Allocation). It is also the same screen as with panelists: when you choose a Carrier Link plan, the screen knows its type and adapts.

  • What it moves: samples from one week to another, within each route, so that there are no overloaded weeks next to almost empty ones. It does not change the plan’s total or its routes.
  • No maximum per panelist: with Carrier Link there are no panelists. The rows are the origin cities and the load is the samples planned per week.
  • Freeze date (fence): from today up to that date, inclusive, nothing is moved. If you leave it empty, the freeze starts today.
  • Draft and active: in a draft plan any week can be moved; in an active plan only pending quotas of weeks that start after the freeze date.
  1. Choose the Carrier Link plan in the plan selector.
  2. Press Balance all origin cities, or Balance on one city.
  3. Review the Balancing preview: how many samples change week. Nothing is saved yet.
  4. Press Apply balancing.
If the distribution is already as even as it can be with what can be moved, ONEMS says there is nothing to move.

Allocation Plans

Screen Allocation Plans (menu E2E ▸ Allocation). When you choose a Carrier Link plan in the selector, the plan’s records are its quotas: one per route (city to city) and week. This view is for consultation; quotas are changed with the Allocation Generator and Load Balancing.

  • Table: one row per quota, with what is planned, what is carried over from earlier weeks, what has been obtained, the requests made to the carrier and its status: Pending, Open, Fulfilled, Carried over or Unfilled. It can be filtered.
  • Matrix: routes in rows and weeks (or months) in columns. Each cell shows what is planned, what is carried over in brackets and, once the plan is running, what has been obtained. The matrix shows all the plan’s quotas: it does not apply the table filters.
  • View samples: on each quota, a link takes you to the samples obtained for that route and week.

If a quota is not filled

If the carrier does not have enough shipments, ONEMS asks again for what is missing (up to 3 times in the same week). Whatever is still missing is spread over the remaining weeks of the month and, if it does not fit, over the following months. What is still missing when the plan ends stays unfilled and raises an alert.

Samples

Screen Samples. It lists the samples obtained, one per shipment.

  • Filters: plan, week, origin and destination city, and “Only possible misrouting”.
  • Columns: shipment identifier, route, start and end readings, transit (in days and hours) and whether it arrived on time.
  • Readings: opening a sample shows all its readings, marked as start, end or intermediate.

The Possible misrouting mark appears when a shipment has readings at more than one point or in a city other than the one requested. The sample still counts towards the route quota, but it is worth reviewing.

This screen is for consultation: samples cannot be edited.

Capture monitor

Screen Capture monitor. Here you follow what happens with data capture.

  • Requests to the carrier: open, fulfilled, partial or failed (including trial ones).
  • Batches received: each batch with its status (Received, Validated, Sampled, Rejected, Undone or Expired), the number of shipments, the rejected ones and the chosen ones.
  • Alerts: with their severity (low, medium or high). You can filter only the open ones and mark them with Mark as seen or Dismiss. What each one means is in What each alert means.

Undoing a batch

If a batch is wrong, press Undo on its row and confirm. The samples chosen in that batch are removed from the E2E reports and the affected quotas are recalculated. ONEMS tells you how many quotas went back to pending and which ones, having already been carried over, need reviewing.

The equivalence time

The carrier’s reading does not include the last stretch to the addressee nor the reporting delays that the panelist measurement does include. To make the two measurements comparable, the delivery standard admits an equivalence time.

  1. Open the delivery standards screen (E2E: SLA).
  2. On the route you want, fill in First-leg equivalence time and Last-leg equivalence time, in the same unit as the standard. They default to 0.
Only for carrier samples. This time is added only to carrier samples; panelist samples do not change. ONEMS keeps the measured time and the added time separately, and the reports (including “on time”) use the total.

Example: if the carrier measures 2 days and the last leg is set at 1 day, the report counts 3 days.

What each alert means

Alerts appear in Capture monitor. These are the types and what to do about each one.

Questions

How many samples can a plan have?
A plan can reach about 15,000 samples a year per product.
Can the carrier choose which shipments it sends?
No. The carrier proposes many candidates and ONEMS makes the final draw. The seed of the draw is fixed before the candidates are received, so nobody can pick it after seeing them.
Is personal data stored?
No. Only the fields on the allowed extra fields list are kept; everything else is discarded on entry.
Who can create connector keys?
Only a superadmin or the UPU Account Manager. The key is shown just once, when it is created.
I applied the plan and no samples are coming in. Why?
Check that the connector is Active (in Testing it only accepts trial runs) and that the day and time in the calendar have already passed. Then look at Capture monitor.
How do I tell these samples apart in the reports?
They carry the origin “carrier”, different from panelist samples, and can be separated.
Do I have to use Carrier Link with every carrier?
No. The capture method is chosen per carrier in Carriers & Products: Panelists, Carrier Link or Both. Carriers that use panelists only keep working as before, and the E2E screens show nothing about Carrier Link for them.
Who sees each screen?
The Account Manager (and the UPU Account Manager) edits plans, points, connectors and the monitor, and sees the samples. The Allocation Plan Manager edits plans and sees the rest except connectors. The DO Coordinator edits reading points and sees the rest except connectors. Panelist Manager and Materials Supplier have no access. A read-only screen shows the read-only label.
