Node Load Balancing

What balancing does, how to use the screen step by step, and how ONEMS decides which day and which node each sample goes to.

What it is and what it is for

The Allocation Plan says which samples must be sent, from which node, to which node and on which day. Over time this distribution gets out of balance: a panelist goes on holiday, a node is left without a panelist, samples are added, or some days have many shipments and others have none.

Balancing reorders the pending samples of each city so the work is shared fairly across days and across panelists, without changing what is measured.

Avoids peaks

No panelist gets many samples at once on the same day or week while others get none.

Respects availability

It does not schedule shipments on non-working days, in the past, or on days when the panelist has said they are away.

Keeps the measurement

It keeps the origin city and the destination city of each sample, and the total number of samples for each month.

You always decide. Balancing first shows you a preview with all the changes. Nothing in the plan is modified until you press Apply balance.

Basic concepts

WordWhat it means here
Sample (shipment)One row of the Allocation Plan: a shipment from an origin node to a destination node on a date. Only pending ones are moved. Sent or received ones are never touched.
NodeA point of the network in a city (for example NAK-01), served by a panelist.
Valid nodeAn active node that has an active panelist. Only valid nodes send or receive samples.
Valid dayA working day (according to the Balancing policies), never in the past, within the plan dates, and a day on which neither the origin panelist nor the destination panelist has declared they are unavailable.
PeriodThe start and end dates you choose in the filters. Balancing only looks at the samples in that period.
FenceA protection date. From today up to that date, balancing does not move, remove or create any sample. It keeps what panelists already have planned for this week unchanged.
Sample to compensateA sample that could not be placed on any valid day (for example, because the city has no panelist). It is taken out of the plan, recorded separately, and balancing puts it back as soon as there is a free slot.
POBOXAn optional rule: every working day, each city must send at least one sample.

Balancing policies

These are the rules of the account. They are saved when you change them and apply to all cities, unless a city has its own rule (see Each city card).

ControlWhat it does
Working daysThe days on which a shipment can be scheduled. Press a day to turn it on (blue) or off (white). The usual setting is Monday to Friday.
Date policyDaily: each shipment is placed on a specific day and the distribution is done day by day. Weekly: only the week is fixed; the shipment goes to the first working day of that week.
POBOXWhen ticked, every working day each city must send at least one sample. POBOX forces the Daily policy. See POBOX.
Fence untilProtection date. By default it is the Sunday of the current week. From today up to that date no sample is touched; balancing starts the next day. You can move it earlier, later or clear it.
POBOX extra samples by productOnly shown when POBOX is ticked. It sets the share of the new samples POBOX creates that goes to each product. The percentages must add up to 100, or all be 0 (then the least advanced plan is used). Press Save.
Overdue samples (pending with a date before today) are never left in the past: balancing moves them to the first valid day after the fence.

Period and criteria

  • Start Date and End Date: the period that is analysed and balanced. You can use Quick Selection: a month, a half-year or the whole year.
  • Reference Samples/Node: how many samples per node and week you consider normal.
  • Deviation Tolerance (%): how far a node can go above the reference before it is flagged.

With these two values, each node × week cell is coloured:

ColourRuleExample with reference 6 and 20%
NormalLess than reference × (1 + %)less than 7.2
HighFrom reference × (1 + %)7.2 or more
SaturatedFrom reference × (1 + % × 1.5)7.8 or more
The reference and the tolerance are only used to colour and warn. The balancing calculation does not use them: it always shares the samples equally among the valid nodes.

Reset goes back to the initial values, including the fence.

Each city card

Each city with samples in the period has its own card. In the header you see the name, the policy applied to it (Daily or Weekly, and POBOX if it is on) and three views.

Recommendations

Below the table, 💡 Recommendations explains in words what ONEMS sees: saturated cells, samples on days of absence, nodes without a panelist, overdue samples, daily peaks and days without shipments with POBOX. The red number next to the title tells you how many important warnings there are.

A city's own rule

Under the city name there are two selectors, Date policy and POBOX. With Inherit from account the city follows the Balancing policies. Choose another value if that city must work differently.

Samples to compensate

Sometimes a sample cannot go anywhere: the origin city or the destination city has no active panelist left, or no valid day remains until the end of the plan. Instead of leaving it on a day when it cannot go out, balancing takes it out of the plan and records it in this block.

  • By origin city: how many are waiting in each city and the most frequent Waiting reason.
  • By plan: for each plan, the total, scheduled, done and to-compensate samples, and the POBOX extra ones (created by POBOX).
Waiting reasonWhat to do
No active panelist at the origin nodeRegister or reactivate a panelist in the origin city.

Plan not activeThe plan of the sample is not active. Activate it if it should continue.
No capacity on valid daysThere is no free slot in the period. Balance with a longer period.
Outside the plan periodNo valid day is left within the plan dates.
What happens next: you do not have to do anything else. Each time you balance that city, ONEMS tries to bring the recorded samples back. The menu shows a counter with the ones still waiting.

Balance one city

  1. Open Allocation › Load Balancing.
  2. Check the Balancing policies: working days, date policy, POBOX and the Fence until date.
  3. In Filters, choose the Start Date and the End Date, for example with the month button.
  4. Find the city card and read its 💡 Recommendations. Use the Day view to see conflicts and empty days.
  5. Press ⚖️ Auto-Balance <city>. The preview opens. Nothing has changed yet.
  6. Review the summary, the warnings and the Before / After matrix.
  7. Press Apply balance to save the changes, or Cancel to leave everything as it was.
What happens next: "Balancing applied successfully." appears and the card is updated. Panelists will see their new dates in the ONE bot on Telegram, with the notices for each day. You can balance again whenever you want: if the city is already balanced, the preview will say so and will not move anything.

Balance all cities

  1. Choose the Start Date and the End Date. Without them the button is disabled.
  2. Press Balance all cities, in the Balancing policies block. The button shows the progress: "Balancing 3/12".
  3. The Balance preview - all cities opens, with one row per city and a total row.
  4. Press Apply balance to apply all the cities, or Cancel.
Each city is applied separately. If one fails ("City … failed"), the others are still applied. Check the row in red and balance that city again on its own.

The calculation in five steps

When you press Auto-Balance, ONEMS does the following for the city, always on the pending samples of the period and starting from the first allowed day: the latest of the start date, today and the day after the fence.

Step 1Fix the impossibleDestinations without a panelist and samples with no possible day.
→
Step 2Share evenlyAcross weeks, days and nodes, without changing the month total.
→
Step 3Bring backSamples to compensate return if there is a free slot.
→
Step 4POBOXAt least one shipment every working day.
→
Step 5Preview or saveAll or nothing.

Samples inside the fence, and sent or received ones, are not part of the calculation.

1 · Fix the impossible

Before sharing out the samples, ONEMS looks at the ones that could not go out as they are:

  1. The destination node has no active panelist. ONEMS looks for another valid node in the same destination city: the one that receives the least that week and, if possible, one that is available that day. The city → city route does not change. In the preview it counts as Reassigned destination.
  2. The origin city has no valid node, or the destination city has none either: the sample moves to Samples to compensate.
  3. There is no valid day for the sample in everything that is left of its plan (not just in the period on screen): it moves to Samples to compensate. If there is a day outside the period, the sample stays where it is and you get a warning, so you can balance with a longer period.
A sample that has something linked to it (retained material, an incident or a tag reading) is never removed from the plan. It stays where it is and the preview warns you with "Samples to compensate that cannot be moved because they are frozen."

2 · Even distribution

This is the heart of balancing. The idea: each month keeps its number of samples (so the seasonality of the plan is respected) and that number is shared equally among the valid slots of the month.

  1. Month → weeks. The month total is split among its weeks according to how many working days each one has within the period. A 5-day week gets more than a 2-day week.
  2. Week → node and day. With the Daily policy, each week is shared equally among the cells (valid node × valid day). With the Weekly policy, it is shared among the valid nodes, and the shipment goes to the first working day of the week.
  3. What does not divide exactly. If one or two samples are left over, they go one by one to different nodes and on different days. The turn also rotates: the next time you balance, they will not fall to the same node.
  4. Move as little as possible. Each cell keeps its samples up to its target (the ones with the earliest date). Only the surplus is moved, to cells of the same month that have less than their share, pairing by date so each sample moves the fewest days possible.
  5. Check each destination. A sample does not go to a day when the destination panelist is away, nor to a node that is both its origin and its destination. If that happens, it is swapped with another sample or goes to the nearest compatible slot.

3 · Bring compensations back

After the distribution, ONEMS tries to return the city's Samples to compensate to the plan:

  1. First they replace POBOX samples. If a node has a POBOX-created sample on a day, the sample to compensate takes its place: same origin node, same day, but with its own destination (its original route). The plan is fulfilled without adding an extra shipment.
  2. If not, they go into a free slot: the day with the fewest shipments at the origin node, on a valid day within the dates of its plan, which must be active.
  3. Limits: at most one compensated sample per origin node and day, and one per destination node and day. They are never placed today, because the notices for the day may already have gone out.
  4. The ones that do not fit keep waiting, with their reason.
A reintroduced sample that is already in a valid slot stays fixed: later balancing runs do not move it.

4 · POBOX

Only if POBOX is on for the city. For each working day with no shipment from the city, ONEMS tries, in this order:

  1. Is it already covered by a reintroduced sample? Then nothing more is needed.
  2. Move: it brings in a pending sample from the nearest day that has 2 or more shipments. It looks first in the same week and then in the neighbouring weeks.
  3. Create: if there is nothing to move, it creates a new sample (POBOX extra).
    • Product: if there is a split by product, the one furthest below its percentage; if not, the least advanced plan that day.
    • Origin: the node of the city with the fewest shipments that week.
    • Destination: a valid node in another city, preferring cities that are already destinations in that plan and the node that receives the least.
  4. If none of this is possible, the day counts as POBOX days uncovered.

Rules that are never broken

  • Only pending samples are moved. Sent and received ones are not touched.
  • Nothing changes from today up to the fence date.
  • Nothing is ever scheduled in the past, on non-working days, or on days when the origin or destination panelist is away.
  • The origin city and the destination city of each sample do not change. At most the node changes within the same city.
  • The total of each month is kept. Only POBOX can add samples, and only a lack of room can set them aside.
  • When you apply, all or nothing is saved: if something does not add up, no sample of that city is changed.
  • If two people apply at the same time on the same city, the second one calculates on the result of the first.

Questions

I balanced and almost nothing changed. Is that normal?
Yes. Balancing moves as little as possible. If the city is already well distributed, the preview says "No balancing needed - the city is already well balanced!" Also check the fence: anything before that date is not moved.
Why are the samples to compensate not being placed?
Look at the Waiting reason. Most often the origin city or the destination city has no active panelist. They will be placed automatically as soon as it has one and you balance again.
Why do the POBOX extra samples not go down after balancing?
Samples to compensate only replace a POBOX sample when the compensation route has a panelist at the origin and at the destination that day. If the destination city has no panelist, the POBOX sample stays.
Can a sample change city?
No. Changing city would change the route that is being measured. It can only change node within the same city.
What happens to this week's samples?
With the default fence (Sunday of this week), they are not touched. If you want balancing to arrange them too, set the fence to an earlier date or clear it.
What is the difference between Daily and Weekly?
With Daily, the distribution is done day by day. With Weekly, only the week matters and all the samples of the week are put on its first working day. With POBOX it is always Daily.
Can I undo a balancing?
There is no undo button. That is why there is a preview: review it before applying. If you are not happy with it, press Cancel.
Who sees this screen?
Admin and superadmin users. Each account sees only its own cities and plans.
